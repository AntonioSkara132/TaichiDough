\begin{abstract}
Learning bimanual manipulation of deformable materials requires models that connect camera observations, tool motion, and material response. We present TaichiDough, a real-to-simulation pipeline for dough observed by one calibrated RGB-D camera while two spatulas follow recorded trajectories. A metric camera--scene calibration and floor-aware reconstruction initialize a mass-preserving three-dimensional Moving Least Squares Material Point Method model. The corrected Taichi simulator replays signed-distance-field tools and differentiates a partial-view depth objective through the full particle trajectory. Material variables are optimized in scaled latent coordinates, while an independent full-resolution evaluator measures visible-geometry prediction. On one recorded sequence, a five-parameter run reduced its training objective by 61.8\% before interruption. A separate completed run improved a strict temporal-continuation score over frozen geometry, but reached the Young's-modulus bound and used mismatch-tolerant CUDA replay. We therefore interpret the estimates as configuration-dependent effective parameters rather than uniquely identified dough constants. The results establish the sensing, simulation, and optimization basis for future synthetic-data generation and robot-controlled bimanual shaping, while making the present identifiability and numerical limitations explicit.
\end{abstract}
