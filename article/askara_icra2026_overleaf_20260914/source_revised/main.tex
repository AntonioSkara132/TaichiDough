\begin{abstract}
Physics-based models for bimanual manipulation of deformable materials must connect camera observations, tool motion, and material response. We present TaichiDough, a real-to-simulation pipeline for dough observed by one calibrated RGB-D camera while two spatulas follow motion-captured trajectories. Metric camera--scene calibration and floor-aware reconstruction initialize a mass-preserving three-dimensional Moving Least Squares Material Point Method model. Registered tool geometries are replayed as signed-distance fields, and material variables are fitted using a differentiable partial-view depth objective. On one recorded sequence, the saved history of a preliminary five-parameter run reduced its training objective by 61.8\% over 15 accepted updates and reached a late-stage plateau. A separate fixed-configuration run improved visible-geometry scores relative to frozen initial geometry during the same recorded motion. Because the experiment uses one view, one trajectory, and no force measurements, the estimates are interpreted as model- and experiment-dependent effective parameters rather than unique dough constants. The results demonstrate an end-to-end observation-conditioned fitting pipeline and identify the measurements and validation needed for future robot-controlled data collection and synthetic-data generation.
\end{abstract}
