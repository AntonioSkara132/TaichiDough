# Numerical foundations, inverse-problem methodology, and manipulation benchmarks

Fresh evidence review for TaichiDough: fitting elastic, viscous, and plastic simulator parameters from depth observations and known tool trajectories. Search/access date: 11 September 2026. This note does not assess the current implementation; a separate code audit is responsible for that. It does not repeat the DPSI/EMPM/PAC-NeRF or food-rheology reviews.

## Main findings

1. **Derivative-free calibration is scientifically legitimate.** Neither differentiability nor a new optimizer is necessary for an empirical identification paper. Arriola-Rios et al. explicitly review parameter fitting by grid search, iterative optimization, and evolutionary methods. Matl et al. provide a direct robotics precedent for depth-based, likelihood-free Bayesian simulator calibration with subsequent physical task tests. The scientific tests are observation agreement, parameter/prediction uncertainty, transfer, and honest model assumptions—not the availability of gradients.
2. **A calibrated parameter and a physically identified parameter are different results.** Kennedy–O'Hagan and Brynjarsdóttir–O'Hagan explain why model discrepancy can be absorbed into fitted parameters. A visually successful effective parameter can be useful without being a material constant. Conversely, narrow apparent uncertainty can be misleading if geometry, contact, or model discrepancy was held incorrectly fixed.
3. **A broad parameter minimum need not invalidate prediction.** Raue et al. distinguish parameters that are uncertain but irrelevant to a particular predicted observable from parameters whose uncertainty changes the desired prediction. Test the range of held-out trajectories generated by all plausible parameter combinations. Do not reject an otherwise accurate predictive model merely because one modulus cannot be uniquely recovered.
4. **Prescribed tool motion alone does not guarantee elastic-scale identifiability.** In a quasistatic, displacement-controlled elastic example with zero body force and homogeneous stiffness, equilibrium is invariant to multiplication of the stiffness by a positive scalar. Depth measures deformation, not reaction force. Dynamics with independently known mass, gravity-driven deformation, known loads, or force measurements can break this ambiguity. This is a derivation below, not a claim that all dough manipulations are unidentifiable.
5. **MLS-MPM, APIC, and snow-style plasticity refer to different layers.** MLS-MPM is a discretization; APIC is the affine particle–grid velocity transfer; the corotated elastic energy and singular-value plastic projection are material-model choices. Calling a code MLS-MPM does not establish that its plastic law is rheologically adequate for flour dough.
6. **SDF geometry is not a friction model.** ILS-MPM explicitly defines nonpenetration, normal penalty, Coulomb bounds, and stick/slip updates. It treats deformable particulate bodies, not just a rigid tool SDF. It is prior art for level-set contact, but not an identical algorithm to every sampled-SDF collision response.
7. **Manipulation success, geometry prediction, and material identification should be separate comparison categories.** RoboCraft/RoboCook/DoughNet are important learned-prediction or planning comparators; they do not identify interpretable dough parameters. PlasticineLab is a synthetic manipulation benchmark, not real calibration evidence.

## Source-selection and verification method

Twelve targeted web searches covered the exact original numerical-method titles, identifiability and discrepancy references, a non-gradient depth-calibration precedent, and the robotic manipulation benchmarks. These were followed by author/publisher/proceedings records and accessible primary full texts. Search results served as leads, not as substitutes for equation-level evidence. Large PDFs that exceeded the WebFetch size limit were read through in-memory PDF text extraction; no source PDF was redistributed.

Evidence labels:
- **Full-text inspected:** relevant original pages/sections were read, including extracted PDF text when appropriate.
- **Primary abstract/project inspected:** bibliography and stated high-level claims were checked; no equation-level claim is inferred from the abstract.
- **Bibliographic/abstract index only:** metadata or abstract was found, but primary full text was not inspected in this review.

This is a focused evidence review, not an exhaustive systematic review. Absence from these searches does not establish a priority claim.

## Fourteen selected sources

### F1. MLS-MPM and CPIC: the original method

**Yuanming Hu, Yu Fang, Ziheng Ge, Ziyin Qu, Yixin Zhu, Andre Pradhana, and Chenfanfu Jiang.** “A Moving Least Squares Material Point Method with Displacement Discontinuity and Two-Way Rigid Body Coupling.” *ACM Transactions on Graphics* **37**(4), Article **150**, 14 pages, August **2018**. Peer-reviewed SIGGRAPH/TOG paper.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/3197517.3201293
- Author page: https://yuanming.taichi.graphics/publication/2018-mlsmpm/
- Primary PDF: https://yuanming.taichi.graphics/publication/2018-mlsmpm/mls-mpm-cpic.pdf
- **Evidence: full-text inspected.** First-page reference block verifies complete bibliography. §1.1 and PDF p.2 separate the MLS-MPM weak-form/stress-divergence discretization from the Compatible Particle-In-Cell (CPIC) extension. §3.2 (PDF p.5) addresses equivalence of APIC/PolyPIC and MLS on velocities. Eq. (29), PDF p.11, combines elastic stress and affine momentum into one matrix–vector contribution during particle-to-grid transfer. §6.1 gives implementation/transfer benchmarks.
- MLS-MPM derives an efficient stress-divergence computation that avoids direct nodal shape-function gradient evaluation. APIC-style transfers are consistent with that discretization.
- CPIC adds colored distance fields and particle/grid compatibility to represent cuts, thin boundaries, and two-way rigid coupling. These are not implied by merely using an MLS-MPM kernel.
- The paper describes particle support spanning a boundary and consequent smoothing in its introduction (PDF p.2). This is relevant to thin tools and contact-resolution studies, but does not prove a specific TaichiDough padding prescription is correct.
- **Implication:** cite for the solver's established basis. Do not claim basic MLS-MPM automatically implements accurate cutting, topology change, or CPIC coupling. Runtime claims from the original paper do not transfer automatically to another implementation.

### F2. APIC: numerical transfer and dissipation

**Chenfanfu Jiang, Craig Schroeder, Andrew Selle, Joseph Teran, and Alexey Stomakhin.** “The Affine Particle-In-Cell Method.” *ACM Transactions on Graphics* **34**(4), Article **51**, 10 pages, **2015**. Peer-reviewed SIGGRAPH/TOG paper.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/2766996
- Primary page: https://disneyanimation.com/publications/the-affine-particle-in-cell-method/
- Primary PDF: https://media.disneyanimation.com/uploads/production/publication_asset/104/asset/apic-aselle-final.pdf
- **Evidence: primary abstract/project inspected; bibliographic record checked.** The Disney page lists all authors, SIGGRAPH 2015, PDF and technical-report links.
- APIC stores a locally affine particle velocity representation rather than only a constant velocity. The abstract reports preservation of angular momentum during particle/grid transfers and reduced PIC dissipation without FLIP's noise/instability characteristics.

### F3. Corotated elasticity and snow-style plastic projection

**Alexey Stomakhin, Craig Schroeder, Lawrence Chai, Joseph Teran, and Andrew Selle.** “A Material Point Method for Snow Simulation.” *ACM Transactions on Graphics* **32**(4), Article **102**, 12 pages, July **2013**. Peer-reviewed SIGGRAPH/TOG paper.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/2461912.2461948
- Primary page: https://disneyanimation.com/publications/a-material-point-method-for-snow-simulation/
- Primary PDF: https://media.disneyanimation.com/uploads/production/publication_asset/94/asset/SSCTS13_2.pdf
- **Evidence: full-text inspected.** First-page reference block verifies title/authors/article/year. PDF p.5, Eqs. (1)–(2), defines a fixed-corotated elastic energy, multiplicative split \(F=F_EF_P\), and plastic-volume-dependent exponential hardening of Lamé coefficients. The same page restricts singular values of \(F_E\) to \([1-\theta_c,1+\theta_s]\); §7 describes the plastic update. Eq. (5), PDF p.5, uses the energy derivative times \(F_E^T\) in the stress-based grid force.
- The material controls critical compression and extension through principal stretches; these are dimensionless thresholds, not automatically yield stresses in pascals.
- The primary abstract describes a user-controllable model for snow, including wet/dense snow, and semi-implicit grid integration. It is not a calibrated wheat-dough rheology paper.
- **Implication:** a similar projection can be a useful phenomenological starting point, but naming a CLI option “plasticity” does not identify which physical yield law it represents. Separate elastic/plastic strain, rate dependence, and hardening in the manuscript. A corotated term multiplied by \(F^T\) is not the first Piola stress itself.

### F4. Level-set contact with an explicit friction law

**Chuanqi Liu and WaiChing Sun.** “ILS-MPM: An implicit level-set-based material point method for frictional particulate contact mechanics of deformable particles.” *Computer Methods in Applied Mechanics and Engineering* **369**, **113168**, **2020**. Peer-reviewed journal article.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2020.113168
- Primary full text (NSF public-access copy): https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10209615
- Publisher: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0045782520303534
- **Evidence: full-text inspected, PDF pp.1–7.** Exact title is considerably more specific than “Implicit Level-Set Material Point Method for Contact and Friction.” §2.1, Eqs. (3)–(5), defines unilateral contact, Coulomb tangential bounds and sticking/sliding. §2.2, Eqs. (6)–(13), gives normal/tangential penalty regularization and return-map-like friction treatment. §3.1, Eqs. (14)–(17), defines sign, normalized gradient normal, and evolving level-set boundary. §3.2 constructs an intermediate candidate contact surface. The abstract and introduction identify deformable particulate/granular assemblies and an implicit solve.

Do not attribute Coulomb friction to another implementation unless its update actually expresses normal-traction-dependent tangential bounds or a stated approximation. Mesh watertightness verifies a geometric property, not physical tool registration, friction, or contact accuracy.

### I1. Structural and practical identifiability

**Andreas Raue, Clemens Kreutz, Thomas Maiwald, Julie Bachmann, Marcel Schilling, Ursula Klingmüller, and Jens Timmer.** “Structural and practical identifiability analysis of partially observed dynamical models by exploiting the profile likelihood.” *Bioinformatics* **25**(15), **1923–1929**, **2009**. Peer-reviewed methodological article.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btp358
- Bibliographic/abstract record: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19505944/
- **Evidence: indexed metadata/abstract checked; full text not inspected.** The PubMed direct fetch encountered a cookie screen. Detailed equations used below are instead verified in I2.
- Relevant concept: uniqueness permitted by equations and observation mapping is distinct from precision permitted by finite/noisy data. Profile likelihood can detect nonlinear parameter trade-offs and support interval estimation.
- **Implication:** use “flat sampled loss” or “weak sensitivity under this experiment” for an arbitrary weighted image score; do not claim mathematical structural non-identifiability solely from a coarse one-dimensional sweep.

### I2. Experiment design and prediction despite uncertain parameters

**Andreas Raue, Clemens Kreutz, Thomas Maiwald, Ursula Klingmüller, and Jens Timmer.** “Addressing parameter identifiability by model-based experimentation.” *IET Systems Biology* **5**(2), **120–130**, **2011**. Peer-reviewed methodological article; biological ODE examples, not MPM.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1049/iet-syb.2010.0061
- Primary full text: https://www.jeti.uni-freiburg.de/papers/ISBEAT_5_2_120_1.pdf
- Record: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21405200/
- **Evidence: full-text inspected, printed pp.120–128.** §1.4 (pp.122–124) distinguishes structural and practical identifiability. §2.2, Eq. (20), p.125, defines the profile by fixing one parameter and minimizing over all others. Figure 4 and text p.126 explain flat, one-sided, and bounded profiles. §3 and Figure 5 (pp.126–127) explicitly distinguish uncertain parameters that do affect required predictions from uncertain parameters with negligible predictive effect. §3.1 (pp.126–127) discusses choosing additional measurements where predictions along the profile differ most.
- **Implication:** one-at-a-time fixed-parameter slices can understate joint uncertainty; they are not full profiles. Flat parameter profiles do not by themselves prove that a particular output cannot be predicted. Evaluate ensembles across plausible parameters on the intended held-out actions.
- A profile of a heuristic geometry loss is informative, but a formal confidence interval needs a defensible likelihood or empirically calibrated threshold; an arbitrary “within 2% of best score” interval is a tolerance set, not a 95% interval.

### I3. Calibration with model inadequacy

**Marc C. Kennedy and Anthony O'Hagan.** “Bayesian calibration of computer models.” *Journal of the Royal Statistical Society: Series B (Statistical Methodology)* **63**(3), **425–464**, **2001**. Peer-reviewed statistical methodology paper.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9868.00294
- Primary record: https://academic.oup.com/jrsssb/article/63/3/425/7083367
- **Evidence: primary abstract and bibliography inspected.** The issue is September 2001. The currently migrated page also shows a January 2002 publication field; cite the issue year **2001**, not the migration/display date.
- The Summary describes Bayesian estimation of unknown code inputs, propagation of remaining parameter uncertainty, and adjustment for model inadequacy revealed by differences from physical observations, including discrepancies after best fitting.
- **Implication:** deterministic sweeps can remain the computational engine while producing a likelihood/tolerance set, a posterior approximation, or a bootstrap distribution. Bayesian analysis is optional, but ignoring model discrepancy can turn geometry/contact errors into apparent material values. Abstract evidence is sufficient for this general point; no uninspected implementation details are claimed here.

### I4. Calibration can predict correctly while missing physical truth

**Jenný Brynjarsdóttir and Anthony O'Hagan.** “Learning about physical parameters: the importance of model discrepancy.” *Inverse Problems* **30**(11), **114007**, **2014**. Peer-reviewed methodological article.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1088/0266-5611/30/11/114007
- Primary record: https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/0266-5611/30/11/114007
- **Evidence: primary abstract and bibliography inspected.** Publication date 28 October 2014.
- The abstract reports biased and overconfident parameter estimates/predictions when discrepancy is ignored, confounding between calibration parameters and discrepancy, and the need for meaningful prior information to distinguish them. In the example, good interpolation prediction does not necessarily imply recovery of the actual physical parameter.
- **Implication:** disentangle claims of good observed-depth forecasts, stable effective parameters, and externally validated rheological quantities. Adding a highly flexible discrepancy term without constraints can also obscure parameter identification.

### I5. A direct non-gradient, depth-based robotics precedent

**Carolyn Matl, Yashraj Narang, Ruzena Bajcsy, Fabio Ramos, and Dieter Fox.** “Inferring the Material Properties of Granular Media for Robotic Tasks.” *2020 IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation (ICRA)*, **2020**. Peer-reviewed conference paper.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/ICRA40945.2020.9197063
- Primary full-text HTML: https://arxiv.org/html/2003.08032v4
- Record: https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.08032
- Author/lab page: https://research.nvidia.com/labs/srl/publication/matl-2020-inferring/
- **Evidence: primary full text inspected through arXiv HTML.** §III states the model assumptions; §IV-A uses BayesSim likelihood-free inference with a Mixture Density Random Fourier Feature network; §IV-B identifies NVIDIA Isaac DEM and solver settings; §IV-C describes 16 shape features; §IV-D describes RealSense D435 sensing. §VI separates simulation and real experiments.
- Fits sliding friction, rolling friction, and restitution from depth-derived pile/ring features. It estimates a Gaussian-mixture posterior, not a simulator adjoint.

## Suggested survey taxonomy

Use two levels rather than treating all “deformable simulation” papers as competitors.

### Level A: what is inferred or evaluated?

| Class | Estimated output | Appropriate evidence | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reconstruction/tracking | Current visible/full geometry | Independent geometric error; held-out camera if possible | Material dynamics or future prediction |
| Forward prediction | Future geometry under recorded actions | Continuous unseen-trajectory forecasts, errors vs horizon | Unique physical parameters |
| Effective simulator calibration | Parameters minimizing a declared score | Better untouched predictions; sensitivity/tolerance set | Recipe-independent rheology |
| Physical identification | Parameters with physical interpretation | Informative experiments, uncertainty, independent mechanical checks | Broad planning success |
| Manipulation/control | Actions reaching a target | Task success, goal error, failures, control/data budget | Exact mechanics or parameter recovery |
| Scientific software/dataset | Reusable procedure/data | Complete measurements, splits, runnable code, independent reproduction | New constitutive or numerical method |

### Level B: how does the inference obtain information?

Code each paper by: observation modality; number of cameras; temporal sequence versus before/after views; known input (tool pose, force, or both); motion class; material family; parameter vector and units; optimizer/inference family; contact assumptions; hidden-volume assumptions; numerical resolution; validation split; uncertainty; release status. Label “not reported/checked” instead of silently marking a capability absent.

Separating **optimizer class** from **experimental evidence** prevents an unfair ranking in which every differentiable paper is treated as scientifically superior to a forward-sweep study.

## Identifiability and measurement design: synthesis for TaichiDough

These are proposed analysis directions, not claims of completed experiments or new theory.

### 1. Observation and simulation model

Write a continuous rollout as

\[
x_{t+1}=\mathcal{S}_{h,\Delta t}(x_t,u_t;\theta,\psi),\qquad
 y_t=\mathcal{H}_{c,o}(x_t)+\delta_t+\epsilon_t.
\]

Here \(\theta\) contains material parameters, \(\psi\) contact/initial-state nuisance choices, \(h,\Delta t\) numerical settings, \(u_t\) measured tool kinematics, \(\mathcal H\) the camera/visibility model, \(\delta\) model discrepancy and \(\epsilon\) measurement noise. They should not all be called “dough parameters.” Numerical settings and contact skin can alter the optimum even when the underlying material is unchanged.

The current scientific opportunity is to ask **which combinations of physical effects can be separated with that observation model and the available motions?**

### 2. An explicit scale ambiguity worth testing

For homogeneous quasistatic linear elasticity, fixed Poisson ratio, displacement boundary conditions, no body forces, and zero traction on remaining boundaries,

\[
\nabla\cdot\left[E\,\widetilde C(\nu):\varepsilon(u)\right]=0.
\]

Multiplying \(E>0\) by any constant does not change the equation for displacement after division by \(E\). Thus a camera observing only displacement cannot recover that absolute stiffness scale in this example; reaction force changes even when observed geometry does not. The assumptions matter: gravity, known loads, inertia with known density, friction/contact force details, and nonlinear/rate effects can change identifiability. This is not a theorem that all depth-based elasticity fitting fails.

Analogous ratio ambiguities can arise when elastic and viscous stress scales are changed together. It is reasonable to ask whether the data identify a relaxation timescale such as \(\eta/E\) more precisely than each parameter separately, but the exact ratio depends on the constitutive law and loading. A fixed displacement hold does not, by itself, let a depth camera measure force relaxation. An informative design may require free recovery, multiple loading speeds, gravity deformation, or a force measurement.

### 3. Distinguishing viscosity from plasticity

A permanent-looking shape over a short observation window can reflect either plastic strain or slow viscoelastic recovery. Include loading, reversal/release, and sufficiently long recovery; repeat similar deformation at distinct velocities and amplitudes. Below-yield and above-yield motion pairs constrain a threshold better than one strongly deforming trajectory. Use small reversible probes before large plastic manipulations where physically appropriate; sample preparation history must be controlled or represented.

A basic Kelvin–Voigt-like rate term, a Maxwell/standard-linear-solid relaxation model, and a rate-independent stretch clamp make different predictions. Sweeping more parameters cannot correct a constitutive family that cannot produce the observed recovery. The model family should be compared rather than assumed identifiable because its parameters are named elasticity/viscosity/plasticity.

### 4. Profiling versus one-at-a-time sweeps

A one-at-a-time curve \(L(E,\eta_0,\theta_{p0})\) measures sensitivity conditional on fixed values. A profile \(\min_{\eta,\theta_p,\psi}L(E,\eta,\theta_p,\psi)\) allows compensation and can be wider. A factorial or space-filling joint sweep followed by refinement can approximate these profiles without derivatives. Interpret an arbitrary loss-tolerance set as a tolerance set, not as a calibrated statistical confidence interval.

Because depth errors and adjacent frames are correlated, many frames from one rollout do not create many independent trials. Use episode- or batch-level resampling; one training episode cannot support an empirical between-episode uncertainty interval. A hierarchical model is an option when specimens share a recipe but differ in preparation.

### 5. Numerical and contact sensitivity

Compare the fitted **predictions** and fitted **parameter values** across at least a controlled range of grid spacing, timestep and particle counts, preserving physical mass, volume, tool trajectory and rendering. Numerical dissipation can compensate for modeled viscosity. Contact projection and repeated velocity attenuation can also change effective dissipation when timestep changes. If changing the number of contacts/updates changes the effect of a per-step velocity multiplier, it is not automatically a timestep-independent physical coefficient.

For SDF padding, report both metres and the ratio to grid spacing. Two experiments answer different questions: hold padding fixed in metres while refining the grid, or hold padding/grid ratio fixed so the padding shrinks physically. Do not call the second “the same physical model” without explanation. Test independent tool-to-marker registration uncertainty rather than using padding to silently compensate for alignment error.

## Falsifiable hypotheses and potential contributions

| Hypothesis | Experiment | What would support it | What would weaken it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Motion diversity separates reversible, rate-dependent and irreversible effects | Compare calibration from one monotonic push with matched-budget loading/release, multi-speed, and multi-amplitude actions | Narrower joint parameter/prediction tolerance sets and improved untouched cross-action forecasts | Parameter trade-offs persist or gains vanish after nuisance uncertainty is included |
| One calibrated depth view is sufficient for a useful restricted predictor | Fit from one view; test on unseen motions and an independent camera/scan not used in fitting | Good visible and independently measured 3-D forecasts for specified actions | Only the fitted view agrees, or hidden-volume errors explain apparent material values |
| A transparent derivative-free search is competitive at small dimension | Equal-data/equal-simulation-budget comparison of coarse-to-fine sweep, random/space-filling search, optional Bayesian optimization | Stable prediction and uncertainty with reasonable compute | Sweep misses correlated good regions or costs much more without gains |
| Some fitted rheological effects are stable across numerical settings | Refit under mesh/timestep/particle/SDF changes with mass and geometry held fixed | Stable parameter combinations and held-out trends | Material viscosity/plasticity values follow numerical dissipation or contact skin instead |
| Effective calibration adds value over defaults | Untouched complete-trajectory tests with frozen-state/default/hand-fit/selected models | Better paired episode-level prediction across more than one motion | Only training improvement or repeated holdout-based tuning |

These are contributions if they produce new and useful empirical results, not because their ingredients are new. A focused study can be scientifically strong without joint recovery of every material parameter or a new MPM solver.

## Fair validation hierarchy

1. **Verification:** conservation where applicable, units, known analytical/synthetic tests, sensor projection, correct tool frames and timestamps, numerical convergence. Synthetic parameter recovery generated with the same model is a necessary diagnostic but can be an inverse-crime test.
2. **Within-episode forecasting:** fit a prefix, propagate continuously, score later observations. This answers future-time prediction under one specimen/session/action history.
3. **Cross-trajectory validation:** fit one or several designed motions, predict new complete tool trajectories with no refitting. This answers action transfer.
4. **Across-specimen and across-batch evaluation:** declare whether the objective is batch-specific calibration or recipe-level transfer. Neither should be confused with universal dough constants.
5. **Independent-observable validation:** extra camera, volume reference or force/torque. Different observables diagnose different failures. Use a likelihood or metric definition that includes visibility, missing support and noise; do not independently align each predicted frame during final prediction scoring.
6. **Task utility, if claimed:** goal attainment and robot success. It is not mandatory for a narrowly framed calibration/prediction paper, but is mandatory if the manuscript claims demonstrated control advantages.

A learned model baseline is necessary only for a comparative learned-versus-physics performance claim. A focused identification paper may reasonably use carefully designed physical/numerical baselines plus the closest identification methods. Conversely, a default MPM model without calibration is not an adequate sole comparator for claiming general superiority to physics-based methods.

## Bounded novelty assessment

The broad idea—identify a physics simulator from visual data and validate physical interactions—is established. Established components do not make the proposed study scientifically empty. A convincing distinction could be an experimentally demonstrated answer to **how far a single depth camera plus known dual-tool kinematics can separate elastic, viscous and plastic behavior in real dough, and which model-parameter combinations remain predictive outside the calibration motions**.

That distinction is strongest if the work reports a parameter–observation–motion analysis, evaluates several constitutive alternatives fairly, separates contact/geometry/numerical nuisance choices from material effects, includes failed or ambiguous identification cases, and releases measurements and scripts. A public dataset would be valuable if it adds controlled action, preparation, metrology, recovery-duration and split information—not merely because it contains more depth frames.

Do not claim “first” from this focused corpus. The defensible publication argument is a clear unanswered empirical question, a specified measurement/model/search protocol, and evidence that changes what another researcher can predict or infer.
